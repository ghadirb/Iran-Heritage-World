## آنچه کودک یاد می‌گیرد
نوروز، به معنای «روزِ نو»، جشن آغاز بهار است. شاهنامه آغازِ آن را به جمشید نسبت می‌دهد (روایتِ اسطوره‌ای)، در حالی که ثبتِ یونسکو و روزِ جهانیِ نوروز واقعیتی مستند است. کودک یاد می‌گیرد این دو را از هم جدا کند.

## Sources
- Primary (to be recorded by a human reviewer): Ferdowsi, *Shahnameh* — the reign of Jamshid and the origin of Nowruz. **Edition, section and lines are NOT yet recorded.**
- Documented layer (checked online, quote-free paraphrase):
  - United Nations, "International Day of Nowruz" — un.org/en/observances/international-nowruz-day: Nowruz means "new day", marks the first day of spring; inscribed by UNESCO in 2009; the General Assembly proclaimed 21 March the International Day of Nowruz in 2010 (resolution A/RES/64/253).
  - UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage: Nowruz inscribed in 2009 and extended in 2016 (ich.unesco.org, element search "Novruz").
- Secondary summaries of the Shahnameh episode (outline only, not citations): Encyclopaedia Iranica (entry on Jamshid), Tehran Times / Middle East Eye retellings, a FEZANA lesson plan. They agree on: Jamshid has a jewelled throne, the divs raise it into the air, the people gather, the day (first of the new year / first day of spring) is named Nowruz. They also say Jamshid's pride later led to his fall to Zahhak (this links to chapter 4 — chronologically earlier).

## Deliberately vague
- Who founded Nowruz: accounts differ (the Shahnameh says Jamshid; other sources name Kiumars or link it to Zoroaster) — the text says "روایت‌ها یکسان نیستند".
- Haft-sin, Chaharshanbe Suri and other customs are not in this chapter until sourced.
- "Equal day and night" is stated as the framing of the equinox only; nothing about exact dates is claimed except the UN's 21 March.
